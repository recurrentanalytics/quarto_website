    Method

    For each period, a generalised extreme value distribution is fitted by L-moments to each year's highest daily value, with one shape for both periods; the 90% range comes from 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the years. Next to it, the plain count: in how many of the years in each period (25 to 30) such a day came. The shape is kept between -0.5 and 0.5, where the L-moment formulas hold.

    • Stations measure at one point. Where a long, openly licensed station record lies within 40 km and 300 m of height and covers both periods, the page uses it: Germany (Deutscher Wetterdienst) and the United States and its territories (NOAA; values that failed NOAA's quality checks are left out).
    • Complete years only. For every source, a year counts with at least 330 days of data, a period with at least 25 such years; the current year never counts.
    • ERA5 elsewhere: a reanalysis on a grid of 0.25 degrees (about 28 km), the nearest land cell. It smooths local extremes, rain most of all, so its rare days are usually milder than a station's. Its daily maximum is the highest of 24 hourly values; days run on UTC shifted by the longitude in whole hours, close to local solar time. ERA5 is used from 1961 on: its earlier years rest on few observations. The last months are ERA5T, a preliminary version that can still change slightly.
    • Month context (for days that are not rare over the year) compares the day with all days of the same month in each period, read off percentiles.
    • At the edge. When a day beats every year of a period, the page says so and gives no number. When the fit puts a day beyond 100 years, or disagrees with the plain count, the page shows the count and the fit's range instead. A return period is how often, on average; it is no forecast.
    • No attribution. The page compares two periods. It does not say why they differ.
    • Not homogenised. Station moves and new instruments are not corrected. Moves of German stations since 1961 (over 0.3 km or 5 m in height) are listed with the place; earlier moves and NOAA's station histories are not checked here.
