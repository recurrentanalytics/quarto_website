LiveLines

An open instrument for one question: when this storm passes, which transmission lines fail, hour by hour, and how much of each substation's load can the remaining grid still serve?

The app is in development and not online yet.

How LiveLines is built

The chain, in six steps

One calculation, always in the same order. Each step is a module you can open, with its formula, its values and what it leaves out.

  1. 1 · Place

    A region or a point anywhere on Earth. Its OpenStreetMap power lines load on demand.

  2. 2 · Storm

    An IBTrACS track turned into the 3-second gust at every line, every hour, with a Holland wind profile.

  3. 3 · Grid

    Buses, lines, plants and hourly load, read from PyPSA files exactly as they are stored.

  4. 4 · Fragility

    A lognormal fragility curve per line, sampled many times: which lines fail, and when.

  5. 5 · Power flow

    A DC power flow that cascades with no operator, against PyPSA's optimum with a perfect one.

  6. 6 · Outcome

    The share of load served per substation and hour, with its spread across the samples.

Start from the place, not the storm

Few people know storm names. Pick where you are, and LiveLines lists every storm that passed nearby, how strong it was there, how often it happens, and which would break the most line.

What it cannot tell you

LiveLines models transmission lines and substations. Storms do most of their damage to distribution lines, poles and service connections, none of which are in the model. A substation that keeps supply can sit in an area that is dark. Every result carries the label transmission level for this reason.

All the limits, in the making-of →

How LiveLines is built →

Comparison
Our DC power flow against PyPSA; our wind against CLIMADA's. Differences are recorded, never tuned away.
Validation
Only a check against observations can make a module validated. None is yet, and the app says so.
Placeholders
Values without a source are shown as such, together with every result that reads them.
Where code runs
The numpy modules run in your browser. Code you write never runs on our server.
